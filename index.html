Case studies

Business context, what I owned, the decisive shift, and the number.

Digital banking · Renmoney

Delivery and operating model under pressure

Context. Digital banking was competing on speed and trust while priorities, handoffs, and backlog growth obscured what was actually strategic.

My role. I owned how work moved from commitment to release: prioritization, planning cadence, and alignment across product, engineering, analytics, and QA.

Problem. Execution was reactive; trade-offs were implicit; stakeholders couldn’t see a single honest picture of what was in flight or why.

What I changed. I redesigned intake and governance, clarified decision ownership, and tightened collaboration so RFCs and delivery rhythm removed ambiguity—fewer misaligned builds, fewer rescue cycles.

Outcome. +34% delivery speed and −63% active backlog—proof the operating system moved, not individual heroics.

HR tech · Leroy Merlin

Internal platforms at retail scale

Context. Internal HR product served 111 stores + HQ and roughly 1,100–1,400 employees—inconsistency and manual work multiplied cost and risk.

My role. I led senior product ownership: roadmap coherence, stakeholder alignment, and execution across a distributed footprint.

Problem. Hiring peaks broke manual processes; acquisition cost was high; experience varied by location.

What I changed. I owned referral and growth mechanics, replaced the dominant manual path with automation where scale demanded it, and standardized execution so regions stopped reinventing the process.

Outcome. +30% candidate inflow, −92% operating cost on the replaced call-center path, +2 Candidate NPS.

Fintech · Tinkoff Bank

Partner ecosystem and rewards

Context. Rewards and partner surfaces had to compound revenue and network effects—engineering and commercial teams needed one clear product direction.

My role. I led product for partner systems and growth levers; I set boundaries and sequencing with engineering and business so “scale” meant repeatable onboarding, not more manual support.

Problem. Partner growth hit operational ceilings; onboarding wasn’t repeatable; duplicate effort across teams burned capacity.

What I changed. I directed the partner platform and workflows toward self-serve; I made product ownership explicit so teams weren’t building the same problem twice.

Outcome. +300 new partners; work contributed to industry-recognized initiatives; foundation for network growth without linear headcount.

Mortgage · Growth

High-volume acquisition and digital journey

Context. Mortgage and acquisition are won or lost on funnel economics, integration risk, and whether operations survive the spike.

My role. I owned the product arc from journey design through integration-heavy delivery (DOM RF and Tinkoff-era growth)—decisions on sequencing, scope, and readiness.

Problem. Slow or brittle journeys; integrations that could stall volume; marketing spend wasted if ops couldn’t absorb demand.

What I changed. I set journey structure, integration-ready requirements, and release order so teams shipped what unlocked volume safely.

Outcome. Up to 60,000 applications in the first month on a major push; mortgage work tied to Global Finance–recognized product exposure.
