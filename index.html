Case studies

Context, role, problem, change, outcome.

Digital banking · Renmoney

Delivery and operating model under pressure

Context. Digital banking needed predictable delivery: competing priorities, unclear handoffs, and backlog growth that masked real strategic focus.

My role. Product leadership on how work flows from strategy to release: prioritization, planning rhythm, alignment across product, engineering, analytics, and QA.

Problem. Execution felt reactive; speed and quality traded off; stakeholders lacked a single honest view of what was in flight and why.

What I changed. Operating model for intake and governance; clearer ownership of decisions; collaboration patterns so RFCs and delivery cadence reduced ambiguity—not “more process,” but fewer wrong conversations.

Outcome. +34% delivery speed and −63% active backlog—evidence that the system of work, not heroics, moved the needle.

HR tech · Leroy Merlin

Internal platforms at retail scale

Context. HR-facing product used across 111 stores + headquarters, roughly 1,100–1,400 employees on internal workflows—errors and friction compound fast.

My role. Senior product leadership: roadmap coherence, stakeholder alignment, and execution across a distributed user base.

Problem. Acquisition and operations were expensive; candidate experience varied by location; manual work didn’t scale with hiring peaks.

What I changed. Referral and growth mechanics; automation where manual processes dominated; standardization so local teams weren’t reinventing the process weekly.

Outcome. +30% candidate inflow, −92% operating cost on the call-center path replaced by automation, +2 Candidate NPS—business, cost, and experience moving together.

Fintech · Tinkoff Bank

Partner ecosystem and rewards

Context. Multi-domain product work: rewards, partner-facing surfaces, onboarding, and growth loops tied to revenue and network effects.

My role. Product leadership across partner systems and growth levers; coordination with engineering and business on what “scale” actually requires.

Problem. Partner growth hit operational limits; manual support didn’t scale; onboarding had to be repeatable.

What I changed. Partner platform direction and workflows so partners could self-serve; clearer product boundaries so teams weren’t duplicating effort.

Outcome. +300 new partners; contribution to initiatives recognized in the industry; foundation for scalable network growth.

Mortgage · Growth

High-volume acquisition and digital journey

Context. Mortgage and acquisition contexts where funnel economics and operational reliability determine whether marketing spend converts.

My role. Own the product narrative from journey design through integration-heavy delivery—DOM RF and Tinkoff-era mortgage growth work.

Problem. Slow or brittle journeys; integration risk; need for volume without collapsing operations.

What I changed. Journey structure, integration-ready requirements, and execution sequencing so teams shipped in the right order.

Outcome. Up to 60,000 applications in the first month on a major push; award-recognized mortgage product exposure (Global Finance).

How I lead

Leadership approach

I lead by making strategy legible to teams: clear bets, explicit trade-offs, and a delivery rhythm that matches reality. I invest in alignment (same priorities across product, engineering, analytics, QA, business) and in systems (how decisions get made, how work enters the funnel, how we know we won). Metrics matter when they reflect something we changed—not when they decorate a slide.

Business clarity → strategic bets → execution design → delivery → measurable outcomes
